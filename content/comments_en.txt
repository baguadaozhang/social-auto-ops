# Format: [tag] text    or plain text (no tag = general)
# Tags: supply / quality / cost / delivery / market / production / digital
# Keep it conversational: questions and first hand experience beat generic praise.

# ---------- supply chain / logistics ----------
[supply] Freight rates are the wild card again this year. Are you locking in long term contracts or staying on spot?
[supply] Curious how many days of inventory you usually carry. We keep ours fairly tight.
[supply] Stability beats a few points of savings, in my experience.
[supply] Do you work with one forwarder or compare quotes each shipment? We moved to long term partners.
[supply] Customs issues come up more often than people expect. Getting compliance right early saves a lot of pain.
[supply] Dual sourcing sounds great on paper, but keeping quality consistent across two plants is the hard part.

# ---------- quality ----------
[quality] Keeping standards consistent across multiple factories is the hardest part of QC.
[quality] What defect rate are you running at? We have been pushing ours down for two years.
[quality] Certification is expensive, but it is a hard gate for the EU and US markets.
[quality] Quality cost and rework cost are really the same line item in the long run.
[quality] How do you set sampling levels? Too tight and cost climbs, too loose and risk climbs.
[quality] Agreed. Money spent on prevention is always cheaper than the fix afterwards.

# ---------- cost / pricing ----------
[cost] Raw material swings hit us harder than labor cost these days.
[cost] Curious how you take cost out without touching quality. We learned that one the hard way.
[cost] If the quote leaves out hidden costs, it always comes back later.
[cost] Agreed. Buying on price alone usually ends up being the most expensive option.
[cost] Scale helps a lot in manufacturing, but small batch orders never get that benefit.
[cost] Do you calculate cost per order or per year? The answer changes a lot with the lens.

# ---------- delivery / lead time ----------
[delivery] Scheduling flexibility matters more than raw capacity when it comes to lead times.
[delivery] What is your on time delivery rate these days?
[delivery] The worst part of a delay is finding out late. Customers forgive problems, not surprises.
[delivery] We ended up rebuilding our scheduling system because of lead time pressure.
[delivery] Reserving capacity before peak season beats paying a premium during it.
[delivery] Sampling and mass production timelines are very different, and customers often mix them up.

# ---------- market / customers ----------
[market] Demand in Europe and the US is moving fast. Which signals are you watching?
[market] Trade shows have split into very different tiers. Picking the right one beats attending many.
[market] Heavy reliance on one big customer is a double edged sword.
[market] Where do most of your new customers come from these days?
[market] Local service capability is becoming the real differentiator in export.
[market] Agreed. Customers are not buying a product, they are buying a solution.

# ---------- production / manufacturing ----------
[production] What payback period are you seeing on line automation? We are evaluating it now.
[production] Process stability affects yield more than how advanced the equipment is.
[production] How long is your changeover time? That is what hurts on small batch orders.
[production] Shop floor standardization is the hardest part, especially with high staff turnover.
[production] Tooling is a big upfront cost, but it pays back over a few years.
[production] Expanding capacity ahead of demand can turn into a burden very fast.

# ---------- digital / automation ----------
[digital] The hard part of digitalization is not the system, it is getting people to actually use the data.
[digital] Are you building your dashboards in house or buying them? We are evaluating options.
[digital] AI is landing faster in quality inspection than in marketing, in my experience.
[digital] Agreed. Tools amplify whatever process you already have, including a messy one.

[digital] Data silos are where these projects usually stall. Cross department buy in is hard.
